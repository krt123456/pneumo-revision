# Learning-science parameters for the Fasih app (A2 to C1 / IELTS 7, 18 months)
Only URLs actually opened are cited. UNVERIFIED = not confirmed from a source I opened.

## Spacing
- Cepeda 2008 (1,350+ participants): best gap is about 20-40% of the delay to the test at 1 week, about 5-10% at 1 year. Rule: as an item's target retention horizon grows, the gap's share shrinks; a too-short gap costs more than a too-long one is NOT verified (UNVERIFIED). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/
- Nakata (SSLA, 128 learners, 20 word pairs): expanding spacing beat equal spacing only slightly; any spacing mattered more. Rule: do not hand-tune expansion; use an adaptive scheduler, and ensure every item gets spaced (never massed) review. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/effects-of-expanding-and-equal-spacing-on-second-language-vocabulary-learning/D1D796306985C52F9BE7A1200AC50DB9
- FSRS (latest on wiki = FSRS-6, 21 weights). Defaults w0-w20: 0.212, 1.2931, 2.3065, 8.2956, 6.4133, 0.8334, 3.0194, 0.001, 1.8722, 0.1666, 0.796, 1.4835, 0.0614, 0.2629, 1.6483, 0.6014, 1.8729, 0.5425, 0.0912, 0.0658, 0.1542. Retrievability R(t,S)=(1+f*t/S)^(-w20), f=0.9^(-1/w20)-1, so R=90% when t=S. Interval I=S/f*(r^(-1/w20)-1). Same-day stability S'=S*e^(w17*(G-3+w18))*S^(-w19). Initial stability S0(G)=w[G-1]. Difficulty: D0(G)=w4-e^(w5*(G-1))+1; D'=D-w6*(G-3)*(10-D)/9; then blend toward D0(4) with weight w7. Recall/lapse stability formulas in the page are the v4 ones (S'r=S*(e^w8*(11-D)*S^-w9*(e^(w10*(1-R))-1)*hardPenalty*easyBonus+1); S'f=w11*D^-w12*((S+1)^w13-1)*e^(w14*(1-R))); FSRS-6 versions of these two: UNVERIFIED (not on page). FSRS-4.5 weights: UNVERIFIED. Rule: ship FSRS-6 defaults, refit weights per user after about 1,000 reviews (refit threshold is my suggestion, UNVERIFIED). https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm
- Desired retention: wiki says workable range is 70-97%, higher = more daily reviews; it gives no single recommended value (0.9 only as example). Rule: default 0.90; for a slow memoriser offer 0.85 as workload relief and cap daily reviews. https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS

## Retrieval practice
- Dunlosky 2013 ratings: practice testing and distributed practice = high utility; interleaving, self-explanation, elaborative interrogation = moderate; keyword mnemonic, rereading, highlighting = low. Rule: every item is tested by recall (typed/spoken), not re-read; spend effort on recall, not passive review. https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/
- Successive relearning (Rawson/Dunlosky review): within a session, retest each item until correct once (feedback after misses, missed items re-queued after other items); repeat in 3+ sessions, at least a day apart (example: two days apart); exam gains over 10 points, over 60% retained after 24 days vs under 20%. Relearning time fell from about 3 min to 1 min per concept by session 3. Rule: new word = same-day correct recall once, then reviews on later days via FSRS. https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-successive-relearning-dunlosky-greve-badali-wissman-rawson.pdf

## Sleep
- Cousins 2020 (52 adults): sleep between two study sessions reduced forgetting of confident facts (d=0.72) but did not help relearning; effect at 1 week not significant. Rule: schedule the new-word session in the evening and a short recall check next morning; do not claim a big benefit. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7953205

## Session length, interleaving
- Short vs long sessions: UNVERIFIED as a direct comparison. Dunlosky rates distributed practice high, interleaving moderate (URL above). Rule: 2-3 short sessions a day (10-15 min); mix skills within a session.

## Habit and motivation
- Implementation intentions: 94 studies, d=0.65 on goal attainment (0.61 starting, 0.77 resisting derailment); must be if-then format. Rule: onboarding asks "If [time/cue], then I study"; store cue and send reminder at that cue. https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/research/constructs/implementation-intentions
- Streaks (Peru RCT, about 60,000 schoolchildren, math app, working paper): streak display raised logins among already active users (+9.4 points weekly logins); personalized reminders worked better for inactive users (+3.8 vs +2.8 first login); no evidence broken streaks discouraged later use; learning effect only suggestive, tiny sample. Rule: show streak plus personalised reminder; send a reminder after a miss. https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf
- Gamification harm (qualitative, 15 interviews + forums): XP/league chasing reduced confidence and interest, caused anxiety. Rule: allow turning off leagues/XP; reward sustained learning not raw volume; skill-matched competition. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175
- Missed-day recovery: no source found, UNVERIFIED. Rule (design inference): one free "freeze" per week; after a gap let FSRS reschedule overdue items instead of dumping all at once; cap the first catch-back day at about 50% of the backlog; no streak-reset shaming.

## Hours and mapping
- Cambridge guided learning hours, cumulative from beginner: A2 180-200, B1 350-400, B2 500-600, C1 700-800 (guidelines only). A2 to C1 is therefore about 500-600 extra hours: about 8-9 hours a week over 18 months (78 weeks), or about 70-80 min a day. https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours
- IELTS and CEFR (official page text): C1 threshold lies between 6.5 and 7; many at 6.5 are C1, institutions wanting certainty require 7; band 8.5+ = C2, 8 borderline; band 5 borderline B1/B2; overall score only, not exact equivalence. Full band table is an image: UNVERIFIED. Rule: target 7.0 overall with a margin; set 7.5 internal mock-test goal. https://www.ielts.org/about-ielts/ielts-in-cefr-scale
